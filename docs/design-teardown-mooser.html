Two ways to hold
a reader's attention

mooserhotel.at earns attention by withholding. esport-manufaktur.vercel.app spends everything on the first screen, then has nothing left. The gap isn't taste — it's restraint, and restraint is a system you can copy.

Reference mooserhotel.at Subject esport-manufaktur.vercel.app Method source audit + 8 live viewport captures at 1440px

The verdict

Your site has one volume knob, and it is turned to maximum on every element. Mooser has a full mixing desk.

85 of your text elements render at weight 900. Almost all are uppercase with −5% letter-spacing. When everything shouts, size is your only remaining tool for hierarchy — so headlines inflate to 108px and the page grows to 9,482px to fit them. Mooser gets the same drama from a serif/sans pairing at ordinary sizes, which leaves weight, case, and contrast free to do the actual work of ranking information.

Findings

Eight things costing you credibility

Ordered by severity. The first two are defects, not opinions.

Critical

Your headline font weight doesn't exist

Every heading on the site asks for weight 900. Your font request stops at 700, so the browser fakes it — smearing the glyphs outward instead of loading a real black cut. On a site whose entire identity rests on heavy type, the heavy type is a forgery.

index.html requests Inter:wght@300;400;500;600;700. The DOM renders 85 elements at weight 900 and 12 at 700.

Fix: add 800;900 to the Google Fonts URL. One line, and every headline on the site sharpens.

Critical

The navigation disappears on half the page

The sticky bar keeps white text and a translucent dark fill regardless of what's behind it. Over your sage sections it becomes near-invisible, and it sits directly on top of your own section headings as they scroll past — so both the nav and the headline lose.

Illegible in 3 of 8 captured viewports — the logo strip, Best Cases, and the contact section. All three are light-ground.

Fix: Mooser's answer is the reason their nav shrinks to a floating dark pill — it carries its own ground, so it never depends on what's underneath. Adopt that, or swap nav tokens per section via IntersectionObserver.

High

One typographic register, used for everything

This is the single biggest aesthetic gap. Mooser's serif carries meaning: it marks a statement. When their sans appears you know instantly you're in UI. You have no equivalent signal, so a card title and a section headline feel identical and only differ in size.

Weight distribution: 900 ×85, 700 ×12, 500 ×28, 400 ×9, 600 ×1. Nearly all display type is also uppercase with tracking-tighter.

Fix: introduce a second face for statements only — headlines and pull-quotes. Not a serif; that's Mooser's world, not esports. A wide grotesque or a technical mono display face gives you the same two-register contrast in a voice that fits gaming.

High

The cookie modal destroys your best asset

Your hero is genuinely good — the character-staggered GSAP build on BEGEISTERN, the masked diagonal grid, the volumetric glow. First-time visitors see none of it. The consent dialog opens immediately and blurs the entire page behind it.

CookiePopup.tsx:51 — backdrop-blur-sm over a full-screen scrim, mounted on load. Your accept button is also #00ff00, a pure green that appears nowhere else in the brand.

Fix: delay the banner until the hero animation completes, or demote it to a bottom bar. Let the intro land first — it's the strongest thing you've built.

High

Six greens pretending to be one brand

Mooser's palette is disciplined to the point of austerity: seven tokens, no signal colours, everything in one family. Your greens were each chosen locally and never reconciled, and #00ff00 — pure display green — sits next to muted emerald in the same viewport.

Across components: #00ff00 ×6, #10b981, #2dd4bf, #84cc16, #00818d, #00dd00. tailwind.config.js has theme: { extend: {} } — nothing is named.

Fix: pick one primary and one accent, name them in Tailwind, delete the rest. Token names make the next inconsistency impossible to add by accident.

Medium

The ground flips six times

Dark, sage, dark, sage, dark, sage. Each flip forces the eye to re-adapt and resets the sense of place — which is why the page reads as six landing pages stacked rather than one document. Mooser holds one cream ground for the entire site and creates depth within it using beige and taupe fills.

Section backgrounds top to bottom: #020617 → #d1dbd2 → transparent → #020617 → #c5d0c6 → #020617 → #d1dbd2. Total height 9,482px.

Fix: commit to dark as your ground — it suits esports and it's where your hero already lives. Get contrast from elevated panels, not from inverting the page.

Medium

Eight corner radii, no scale

Mooser has exactly two radius tokens plus one deliberate exception — form fields stay square, which makes them read as functional rather than promotional. That's a rule doing real work. Your eight values are the fingerprint of decisions made card by card.

In the live DOM: 9999px ×46, 32px ×21, 40px ×7, 20px ×4, plus 24, 28, 48 and 51.2px.

Fix: three tokens — pill for controls, one card radius, one large-surface radius. Square for inputs.

Medium

Your second-largest headline isn't English

"BRANDS WE LEVELING UP." is set at 108px — the biggest type on the site after the hero. It sits directly above Mercedes-Benz, Indeed and Sparkasse logos, which is precisely the moment you're asking to be taken seriously.

Rendered at 108px / weight 900 / −5.4px tracking, immediately above the client logo strip.

Fix: "Brands we level up." Or drop the English entirely — the rest of the page is German, and the switch buys you nothing.

Translation, not imitation

What to take — and what would be a mistake

Mooser sells stillness to people booking an alpine retreat. You sell energy to brand managers buying esports activations. Copying their surface would read as costume.

Take the structure

Portable

  • Two type registers. A display face that means "statement", a UI face for everything else.
  • A radius rule with a deliberate exception that carries meaning.
  • One ground, held. Depth from fills, not from flipping the page.
  • Split motion. Slow one-shot reveals for content, fast springy easing for interaction.
  • A nav that carries its own background so it never depends on the section behind it.
  • Palette austerity. Seven named tokens is a feature, not a limitation.

Leave the surface

Not portable

  • The brown-and-cream palette. Warm earth tones read as spa, not competition.
  • The Didone serif. Elegant for a chalet; wrong for a stage with 4,000 people at it.
  • 50–120px radii everywhere. Too soft for your subject; you'd lose all edge.
  • Handwriting accents and joke marquees. Charming from a hotel, unserious in a B2B pitch to Mercedes.
  • The multi-second cinematic intro. Yours already lands faster, which is right for this audience.

Implementation

The token layer you're missing

Every finding above traces back to one cause: nothing is named, so nothing can be reused. This is the file that fixes six of the eight.

// tailwind.config.js — currently: theme: { extend: {} }
theme: {
  extend: {
    colors: {
      ground: '#020617',        // the one ground, held
      raised: '#0B1628',        // panels — replaces the sage flip
      line:   '#1C2B44',
      brand:  '#10b981',        // one primary
      accent: '#2dd4bf',        // one accent
      // delete: #00ff00, #00dd00, #84cc16, #00818d, #d1dbd2, #c5d0c6
    },
    borderRadius: {
      card:    '24px',          // every card
      surface: '40px',          // large panels only
      // controls use rounded-full; inputs stay square
    },
    fontFamily: {
      display: ['"Archivo Expanded"', 'Inter', 'sans-serif'],  // statements
      sans:    ['Inter', 'sans-serif'],                        // everything else
    },
  },
}

// index.html — the one-line critical fix
- family=Inter:wght@300;400;500;600;700
+ family=Inter:wght@300;400;500;600;700;800;900

Sequence

Do it in this order

  1. Load the 900 weightOne line. Immediately sharpens every heading on the site. Ten minutes.
  2. Fix the navigation contrastGive the bar its own ground. Removes a genuine legibility failure on three sections.
  3. Delay the cookie bannerLet the hero animation finish first. Your strongest asset currently never gets seen.
  4. Write the token fileColours, radii, fonts. Collapses six greens and eight radii into a system, and stops the drift recurring.
  5. Commit to the dark groundRemove the sage sections; rebuild that contrast with raised panels. The largest visual change, and the one that makes it feel like one site.
  6. Add the second type registerA wide or technical display face for statements only. This is what finally closes the gap with Mooser.
  7. Fix the English headlineFive minutes, and it's sitting above your best client logos.
